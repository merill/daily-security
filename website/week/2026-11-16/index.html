Week in brief

Zero Trust guidance centers AI security on foundations, agents, and agentic insiders

This week brought a coordinated refresh of Zero Trust guidance for AI-era security. The “Security for the AI era” page now organizes the guidance around three priorities—secure foundations, defend with agents, and secure the agentic insider—while related pages expand the treatment of AI-amplified exposure, autonomous attacks, guardrails, accountability, and AI-dependent assets. A new page also maps Microsoft’s Secure Future Initiative to Security Adoption Framework guidance.

  • Zero Trust · AI and agents
    AI-era guidance adopts three security priorities

    The retitled and substantially revised “Security for the AI era” guidance now focuses on AI-amplified exposure, autonomous attacks, and agentic-insider risk through three priorities: secure foundations, defend with agents, and secure the agentic insider. Links and metadata were also updated.

  • Zero Trust · Fundamentals
    Zero Trust overview adds AI guardrails and agent accountability

    The overview now emphasizes AI-amplified exposure, increasingly autonomous attacks, and agents as insiders. It adds guidance on guardrails, playbooks, and accountability, links to Microsoft Perception, and updates its Security Adoption Framework reference.

  • Zero Trust · General
    Foundation guidance expands outcomes for AI-dependent environments

    “Secure your foundations” is now framed around protecting the identities, data, applications, infrastructure, networks, endpoints, and operations that AI depends on. It adds expected outcomes covering visibility, data protection, identity governance, approved development practices, exposure measurement, risk ownership, and continuous improvement.

  • Zero Trust · General
    New SFI guidance maps initiative areas to SAF disciplines

    The new page explains how Microsoft’s Secure Future Initiative informs Security Adoption Framework guidance through culture, governance, security principles, engineering priorities, and implementation practices, and maps SFI areas to relevant SAF security disciplines.

For security architects and administrators

Security architects and administrators can use the revised priorities, expanded readiness outcomes, and SFI-to-SAF mappings to shape AI security planning, agent oversight, governance, and modernization work. The supplied guidance states no required administrator action.
