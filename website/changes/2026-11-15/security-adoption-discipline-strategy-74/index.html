Security Adoption Discipline Strategy

15 November 2026Doc update+16 / −7 lines

In brief

The guidance adds NIST CSF, SAF, CIS Controls, and Microsoft benchmark links; clarifies role ownership; adds KPI/OKR measurement, broader risk dimensions, change and problem management, and links to critical assets and lifecycle decisions. It also expands references to posture management, SecOps, incident operations, and related security disciplines.

What security teams need to know

Administrators gain clearer guidance for assigning accountability, measuring security roadmaps, integrating risk, and connecting governance decisions to operational work. No administrator action is stated as required.

Within SAF, this discipline is a planning and oversight function that aligns security outcomes to business priorities, risk tolerance, and long-term operational sustainability.

For cross-framework traceability, map this discipline to NIST CSF 2.0 and SAF guidance and CIS Controls and Microsoft benchmarks. For execution pathways tied to business impact, pair strategy decisions with the secure critical assets scenario and the privileged access scenario.

Why this discipline?

Many organizations approach security governance through traditional governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) models that prioritize audits and external compliance. While necessary, these classic GRC approaches often fail to manage risks of real-world incidents that cause that operational disruption, data loss, recovery costs, and reputational damage. 4. Align security efforts across disciplines and initiatives Ensure that architecture, operations, and engineering efforts work toward shared outcomes rather than operating in isolation. 5. Measure progress and continuously improve Use metrics, risk insights, and operational feedback to track effectiveness, drive prioritization, and refine strategy over time.

Clear decision rights and accountable ownership are required at every step. For role model guidance, see Security roles and responsibilities in SAF.

Manage organizational change

This discipline helps organizations shift from checkbox compliance toward business‑aligned risk management, while still meeting regulatory obligations. Primary roles commonly include:

  • Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
  • Business Information Security OfficersOfficer (BISO)
  • Security DirectorsDirector/Manager
  • Security Architects

These roles are supported by functions such as security strategy, integration and governance, education and engagement, insider risk management, security posture management, and security compliance management.
